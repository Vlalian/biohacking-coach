# User Stories & Journeys

Full framework reference: [REFERENCE.md](REFERENCE.md)

## Quick start

Ask the user: "What product idea or feature do you want to map?"  
Then work through the three-story sequence below — or jump to the right layer based on what they need.

---

## The Three-Story Sequence (Lichaw Framework)

Every product has three nested stories. Work top-down; each story informs the next.

### 1. Concept Story — *What is the product?*
Maps how users THINK about and see value in the product. Start here for new ideas.

Answer each plot point in order:
- **Exposition**: Who is the user? What is their world? What is their big goal?
- **Inciting Incident**: What problem or need disrupts their status quo?
- **Rising Action**: What is this product (name + category)?
- **Crisis**: What is the competition? What mental hurdles or resistance exist?
- **Climax**: What is the core value proposition / differentiator? (The "BOOM" moment)
- **Falling Action**: What do users THINK or ENVISION after learning about it?
- **End**: User sees themselves achieving their goal. Business mission is served.

> **Check**: Is there a genuine crisis + climax? If the story is a flat line, it's anticlimactic — push for more conflict and a stronger value prop.

---

### 2. Origin Story — *How do users become customers?*
Maps the discovery-to-first-use journey. Use for marketing, onboarding, landing pages.

- **Exposition + Inciting Incident**: Same as concept story
- **Rising Action**: Acquisition channels (search, social, word-of-mouth, ad, email)
- **Crisis**: Resistance — why might they NOT take action? (competitor, friction, confusion)
- **Climax**: Where do they land and what do they SEE? (home page, App Store, landing page) — what makes them care?
- **Falling Action**: The ONE primary action you want them to take (sign up, try demo, call)
- **End**: First conversion. Business acquisition goal met.

> **Cliffhanger test**: If your funnel has a drop-off point, that's where the story ends prematurely. Fix the cliffhanger.

---

### 3. Usage Story — *How do users experience the product?*
Maps in-product flows. Choose the right scope:
- **Epic** — full lifecycle (weeks/months/years)
- **Serial** — recurring sessions (each episode must get better)
- **Micro-story** — single core task or flow

- **Exposition**: User's goal in this session/context
- **Inciting Incident**: What triggers this journey? (CTA, notification, need)
- **Rising Action**: Step-by-step user actions
- **Crisis**: Friction, confusion, paywall, boredom — what might block them?
- **Climax**: The "aha" or "BOOM" moment — core value experienced
- **Falling Action**: Quick closure (don't leave them at the peak)
- **End**: Goal met. What's next? (sequel hook = retention driver)

## Agile User Stories

Once the usage story is mapped, convert each meaningful step into buildable user stories:

```
As a [type of user], I want [to do something] so that [I get some benefit].
```

**Acceptance criteria** (Given-When-Then):
```
Given [context], when [action], then [observable outcome].
```

**User Story Map** (Patton): Arrange stories on two axes:
- X-axis: user activities in chronological order (the backbone)
- Y-axis: tasks/stories under each activity, priority top to bottom
- Draw horizontal slice lines to define MVP and future releases

---

## Journey Map

Complement the story with a journey map table covering:

| Phase | Action | Thought | Emotion | Touchpoint | Pain Point | Opportunity |
|-------|--------|---------|---------|------------|------------|-------------|

Standard phases: Awareness → Discovery → First Use → Ongoing Use → Advocacy

## Finding the Story

When the user is unsure, use these techniques:
1. **Listen** — interview users; ask "What if this worked like magic?"
2. **5 Whys** — ask "why?" recursively to find the real goal beneath the stated want
3. **Measure** — look at funnel analytics; drop-offs are cliffhangers
4. **What If?** — brainstorm ideal/magical versions; map them as hypotheses
5. **Borrow** — reverse-engineer successful products' stories

---

## Output Formats

Adapt output to what the user needs:
- **Story arc diagram**: narrative arc with labeled plot points (describe in text or suggest a tool)
- **Story document**: written paragraph per story type (good for alignment/pitches)
- **Elevator pitch**: "For [user] who has [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [competition], it [differentiator]."
- **User story list**: numbered "As a / I want / So that" with acceptance criteria
- **Journey map table**: phases × rows (actions, thoughts, emotions, touchpoints, pain points, opportunities)
- **Story map**: backbone activities with story stubs underneath, release slices marked

## Rules of Thumb

- The USER is always the hero — never the product or business
- No conflict = no story = no engagement (flat lines are bad)
- Goals are measurable — define qualitative + quantitative success criteria
- Ask "why?" until you find the real goal
- Test every story — look for the genuine smile at the climax (the "smile test")
- Plan for branching paths — not everyone takes the happy path
