# Domain Glossary

This file defines the canonical terms for the Biohacking Coach App. Use these terms exactly in issues, PRDs, code, and agent prompts. Do not drift to synonyms.

## User Stories

**US-1 — First session orientation**
As a first-week athlete, I want the Coach to tell me what to do next at the end of our first session, so I don't have to figure out the app on my own.
_Given_ the athlete has just agreed their first Week Plan, _when_ the Coach delivers the Coaching Closing Move, _then_ it names the Training Plan, Equipment tab, Glossary, and Coach Chat with a concrete example of when to use each — in plain language, as part of the send-off, not as a list.

**US-2 — Mid-week Coach access**
As an athlete mid-week, I want to ask my Coach a question and get an answer that knows my context, so it feels like a real coaching relationship rather than a chatbot.
_Given_ the athlete opens Coach Chat at any point in the week, _when_ they send their first message, _then_ the Coach responds with awareness of their name, race target, current Training Phase, and Week Plan — without the athlete having to re-introduce themselves.

**US-3 — Skipped session safety**
As an athlete who skipped a session, I want to know the plan isn't broken, so I don't feel like I've failed and abandon the week.
_Given_ the athlete marks a session as skipped on the calendar, _when_ they next open the app, _then_ there is no alarm, no prompt to explain themselves, and no visible disruption to the rest of the Week Plan. Context is gathered at the next Weekly Session.

## MVP Success Metrics

**Retention Past Day 10** — baseline engagement metric. The first 10 days are the habit formation window (per Fogg/Clear). Retention beyond this point signals the daily ritual has taken hold.

**Coach Engagement Rate** — the ratio of active training weeks where the athlete initiated at least one meaningful Coach interaction beyond the mandatory Weekly Session. Counts any of: a Coach Chat conversation, a Session Negotiation triggered by a plan deviation, or an extended discussion within the Weekly Session. A high rate signals the athlete is treating the Coach as an accessible coaching relationship — asking questions, seeking alternatives, reflecting on performance. A low rate signals passive compliance: the athlete receives the plan and executes without engaging. This is the primary engagement health metric for the product, reflecting its core value proposition of an interactive, high-quality coach rather than a training schedule generator.

**Qualitative Feedback** — collected via post-Weekly Session micro-surveys (one rotating question per session: Coach attunement, plan fit, trust signal) and structured exit interviews with early users. Key interview questions: "When did it feel like a coach, and when did it feel like an app?" and "What would have made you quit?"

**Trust Signal** — the single most valuable qualitative data point: "Would you have done something different if you'd decided alone?" Measures genuine delegation to the Coach vs. passive compliance.

## Coach Intelligence

**Declared Uncertainty** — the Coach explicitly naming when its signals conflict or its history with the athlete is insufficient to make a confident call. Example: "Your sleep looks fine but your pulse is elevated — I don't have enough history with you yet to read that confidently. What's your gut?" Strengthens Peer Authority by demonstrating honesty over performance. Informed by HI Pattern 4 (Seamful Interaction Design).

**Reliance Calibration** — the Coach's continuously updated internal model of how to weight athlete self-reports against Contextual Signals. Updated based on observed consistency between what the athlete reports, what the data shows, and what outcomes follow. Never surfaced to the athlete as a score or metric — operates invisibly. Informed by HI Pattern 10 (Adaptive Trust Architecture).

## Coach Posture

**Peer Authority** — the Coach's communication posture. Confident, evidence-led, and direct. The Coach states its read of the data and its recommendation without hedging, then genuinely invites athlete input as a peer contribution — not as a politeness gesture. The Coach holds its position unless given real reason to change it. It does not defer, does not apologise for its conclusions, and does not position the athlete as the superior authority. Structure of a recommendation: (1) state the evidence, (2) state the conclusion, (3) invite athlete response. Example: "Three hard days, short sleep, pulse elevated. Easy day. What's your take?" Informed by HI Pattern 8 (Human Expertise Amplification).

## Product Positioning

**Achievement Motivation** — the primary motivational driver of the target athlete. Ironman trainees are driven by improvement and performing at a high level. All Coach interactions, Trajectory Projections, and UX decisions should reflect and feed this drive — not just provide comfort or calm.

**Experience-Adaptive UX** — the principle that the product's emphasis and interaction style shift based on athlete experience level. Veterans receive the Coach's plan first and react if something doesn't fit — they know their bodies and need minimal hand-holding. Beginners need more structure, more explanation of the reasoning behind the plan ("you might have heavy legs but this is the week before recovery — that's intentional"), and more trust-building in the Coach's programme. In terms of motivational framing: beginners see aspiration (what consistent training can unlock), veterans see personal progression (their own trajectory and history). Connects to Progressive Disclosure and Trajectory Projection.

## Agents

**Coach** — the user-facing agent. Holds full individual context, leads all athlete interactions, drives Session Negotiation, and initiates Progressive Disclosure and Trajectory Projection. The central coordinating agent.

**Knowledge Oracle** — the research agent. Holds Ironman training science, periodization, physiology, and nutrition timing. Receives only anonymised queries — never personally identifiable information.

**Tone Adaptation** — internal mechanism by which the Coach adjusts its communication style to match the athlete's current emotional state, motivation profile, and experience level. Not a separate agent — a processing step on every Coach output. Two dimensions: (1) emotional state adaptation (reads current Check-in and adjusts warmth and urgency), (2) persona adaptation (adjusts vocabulary, technical depth, and response length based on Communication Style in Athlete Profile).

**Communication Style** — a field in the Athlete Profile capturing how the athlete prefers to be coached. Seeded during Onboarding Session. Drives Tone Adaptation across all Coach interactions. Examples by archetype: beginner needs warmth + plain language + normalisation of uncertainty; veteran needs terseness + technical vocabulary + no reassurance; returning athlete needs calm confidence + injury-aware framing; analytical athlete needs reasoning-first responses with training science explained.

## Coaching Hierarchy

**Hyper Intelligence** — the product's foundational claim: a Head Coach and the Coach together produce coaching neither could alone. Downward, the human contributes what data cannot see — technique observed at the pool, life context, race craft — entering the plan as Prescribed Sessions and corrections. Upward, the Coach Briefing gives the human an analyst that has read every data point. The athlete experiences one coherent plan, narrated by one voice, backed by two intelligences. In solo mode the claim holds in reduced form: the Coach alone fuses the athlete's subjective signals with training science (Knowledge Oracle).
_Avoid_: HI, human-in-the-loop, hybrid coaching, AI+human synergy

**Head Coach** — the human coach overseeing a Roster of athletes. The highest coaching authority for any athlete they are linked to — outranks the Coach on coaching decisions. Editor-in-chief of the plan: the Coach still drafts the Week Plan through the Weekly Session, but the Head Coach may add, edit, or delete sessions at any time, and Head-Coach-authored content is never silently modified by the Coach or its automation. Optional by design: the product is fully functional without one. Athlete-facing surfaces show the Head Coach's real name; "Head Coach" is the domain and coach-facing term.
_Avoid_: parent, human coach, trainer, mentor

**Roster** — the set of athletes linked to a Head Coach.
_Avoid_: children, clients, trainee list

**Coaching Link** — the one-to-one relationship between a Head Coach and an athlete. Created via a single-use, short-lived invite code — either side may initiate, the other must explicitly accept. Trust in *who* the Head Coach is lives out-of-band: the athlete knows them in real life. No public coach discovery, no marketplace, no credential verification — the app's only job is guaranteeing the link lands on the intended person and cannot be misused by anyone else. Severable by either side at any time; severing cuts all access, including history. An active Coaching Link is what puts an athlete in Coached Mode.
_Avoid_: parent-child relationship, pairing, follow

**Link Visibility** — the athlete-controlled, per-section sharing settings on a Coaching Link, adjustable at any time in Settings. The relationship is doctor-patient shaped: the Head Coach is a professional whose job includes confidentiality — the app supplies boundaries and choice; the humans own the responsibility. Sections and defaults: calendar, sessions, statuses and move log (always on, non-toggleable — a Head Coach who can't see the plan isn't a coach; sever the link instead); Session Reflections (on); Check-in data (on); Athlete Profile training fields and stats (on); Coach briefings (on); Coach Chat and Weekly Session transcripts (off — shareable, but consciously opt-in: a transcript is what the athlete told the AI unguarded).
_Avoid_: privacy settings, data sharing consent, permission matrix

**Coached Mode** — the app state of an athlete with an active Coaching Link. Solo athletes (no link) experience today's product unchanged; nothing in the core experience assumes a Head Coach exists. In Coached Mode the Coach narrates every Head Coach action to the athlete with attribution ("Lars added a brick session Thursday — he wants you race-sharp") — the plan never mutates silently by an invisible hand. Human-to-human messaging between Head Coach and athlete is deliberately out of scope for MVP (they know each other in real life); gathering that conversation into the app is a later product stage.
_Avoid_: B2B mode, supervised mode, managed athlete

**Roster View** — the Head Coach's home surface, reached via a Roster entry that appears in the Navigation Drawer when an account holds active Coaching Links. Head Coach is a *role* on a normal account, not a separate app — the same person can hold a Roster and train as an athlete. Lists each linked athlete with at-a-glance state (name, race target, Training Phase, days to race) plus anything waiting for the coach: pending rebalancing suggestions on Prescribed Sessions, dangling unavailable sessions, new Coach Briefings. Tapping an athlete opens their Training Plan calendar — the same component the athlete uses, rendered with Head Coach permissions. Deliberately scope-limited: no aggregate analytics or athlete-comparison dashboards — the Coach Briefing is the differentiator, not charts. Coached Mode ships in V1, not MVP: it is the first feature requiring server-side shared state, and MVP metrics are solo-athlete metrics. The POC may fake the coach surface with a mock roster.
_Avoid_: coach dashboard, admin panel, client list

**Coach Briefing** — the Coach's reporting channel to the Head Coach about one athlete: Pattern Insights, Pushback Rationale synthesis, and week summaries, delivered in the Coach's voice as coaching observations, never raw scores. Conversational, not broadcast — the Head Coach can interrogate it ("how has her sleep trended?", "draft me a substitute for Thursday") and the Coach answers strictly within that athlete's Link Visibility. The upward half of Hyper Intelligence: the human coach gains an analyst that has read every data point.
_Avoid_: report, dashboard, analytics feed

## Rituals

**Check-in** — the opening phase of the Weekly Session. The athlete reports bodily state for the week — perceived energy, physical condition, sleep quality, and any notable signals. Happens once per week, not daily. The Coach reads Check-in signals alongside Session Feedback from the preceding week to build the following week's plan.

**Session Priming** — optional pre-training ritual. Prepares the athlete mentally and confirms session intent before training begins. Distinct from the weekly Check-in.

**Session Reflection** — the post-session ritual. Captured via two RPE 1–10 ratings (one for Body Feedback, one for Mind Feedback) with a visual illustrative element, plus an optional free-text comment. The comment field may include carbohydrate intake notes — a common use case for serious athletes. Requires no conversation — the athlete taps and optionally types. Two entry points: (1) a Session Feedback Prompt surfaces when the athlete marks a session complete; (2) the Training Plan calendar allows viewing and editing the rating at any time by expanding the completed session day. The Coach reads accumulated Session Reflections as part of the Weekly Session to inform the next Week Plan.

**Session Feedback Prompt** — a popup surfaced when the athlete marks a session complete via a "Mark complete" button on a Planned Session in the Training Plan calendar. Presents two RPE 1–10 ratings (Body Feedback, Mind Feedback) with visual element, and an optional comment field. Comment field may be used for training notes or carbohydrate intake logging. Designed to be fast — under 10 seconds to complete. The submitted rating is stored against the session and remains editable by expanding the session in the calendar. V2: triggered automatically via external API when workout completion is detected from a wearable or fitness platform.

**Onboarding Session** — the structured first meeting between Coach and athlete. Three design principles: (1) **Fast to start** — a structured MCQ screen captures key profile fields (experience level, age, race target, weekly availability, communication style) quickly before any conversation begins; (2) **The differentiator** — the Coach's follow-up clarifying questions after the MCQ are what distinguish this app. They are specific and insightful, not generic. This is where the coaching relationship begins; (3) **Experience-adaptive branching** — the question path forks based on experience level. Beginners do not see the same questions as experienced athletes, and vice versa. Specific MCQ field set to be defined with reference to a best-in-class onboarding example (deferred). Seeds the Athlete Profile with fitness level, training history, race target, motivations, and communication preference.

**Historical Data Upload** — a cold-start accelerator available during Onboarding for athletes who identify as experienced. The athlete exports training history from Garmin Connect (or similar) and uploads it. The app reads the data to pre-populate training history and experience signals in the Athlete Profile, reducing the number of onboarding questions needed. File format: .fit or .gpx (MVP). Replaces manual training history questions for experienced athletes. V2: replaced by live Garmin Connect API integration.

## Privacy

**Privacy Proxy** — the on-device layer that constructs anonymised Knowledge Oracle queries from structured athlete data (Training Phase, Check-in state, Body Feedback, Mind Feedback). Ensures no personally identifiable information leaves the device. In MVP, free-text from the athlete never reaches external LLMs — only structured fields are transmitted.

**Session Context** — the curated, minimal set of relevant Athlete Profile history assembled on-device by the local orchestration layer and injected into each Coach prompt. The hosted LLM never accesses the raw local store — it only sees what the device explicitly chooses to include. Architectural expression of the GDPR data minimisation principle.

## Training Plan

**Training Plan** — the Coach's rolling view of the athlete's upcoming training, presented as a monthly calendar grid with a Coach narrative alongside. The athlete can self-service relocate sessions to different days via Session Move — silently within the week, or across weeks as a Cross-Week Move (gated early on by the Move Checkpoint). The grid shows 4–5 weeks as rows, with days of the week as columns (Mon–Sun). The calendar is the dominant element — the athlete can see how load is distributed across the month and look ahead at the full training block. The Coach narrative contextualises the current week: where the athlete is in their Training Phase, what the block is building toward, and why. The plan is Coach-owned but athlete-influenced: it is not a locked schedule. Changes cascade forward via Week Rebalancing.

Each day in the grid carries a colour-coded dot indicating the session type and status:
- Dot colour = session type (e.g. endurance, intensity, tempo, recovery, rest)
- Dot style = status: solid = completed, outline = planned, muted/crossed = skipped
- Past days are slightly greyed out; future days display at full opacity.
This allows the athlete to read training load distribution and session variety at a glance without opening individual entries. Tapping a day expands it inline to show the full Planned Session detail: session type, parameters, and the Coach's one-line note. Tapping again collapses it. The athlete can navigate backwards into past months (training log) and forwards into future planned months. Past months show completed, skipped, and modified sessions as a training record. Future months show Planned Sessions as far ahead as the Coach has built the schedule, becoming progressively sparser further out.

**Week Plan** — the Coach's recommended session schedule for the current 7-day window, derived from Training Phase and the athlete's accumulated history. Each entry is a Planned Session. When a Planned Session is completed, modified, or skipped via Session Negotiation, the Coach rebalances the remaining Planned Sessions accordingly. The Week Plan rolls forward continuously — it is never static.

**Planned Session** — a session recommended by the Coach for a future day, as part of the Week Plan. A Planned Session is a proposal, not a commitment. It becomes a real session only once the athlete enters Session Negotiation on that day. If context changes (fatigue, illness, schedule conflict), the Planned Session may be modified or redistributed by the Coach across the remaining days. Each Planned Session entry shows: day, session type, key parameters (duration, intensity zone), and a one-line Coach note explaining the reasoning. The Coach note is what differentiates the calendar from a generic training schedule.

**Prescribed Session** — a session authored by the Head Coach for an athlete on their Roster. Sits in the same Week Plan and calendar as everything else, subject to the same athlete physics: draggable (Session Move and Move Checkpoint apply), skippable, Rest dominance applies, immutable once completed. Its content is read-only and undeletable for the athlete — and for the Coach: the AI may *suggest* changes to a Prescribed Session (e.g. from Week Rebalancing) but never applies them; the Head Coach decides. The Head Coach may also edit or delete Coach-authored Planned Sessions — outranking the AI — but may only view Athlete Sessions: the athlete's own entries are their territory. Authority summary: placement belongs to the athlete, content belongs to the author, the Head Coach outranks the AI but not reality.
_Avoid_: coach workout, assigned session, manual session

**Week Rebalancing** — the Coach's adaptive response when a Planned Session is skipped or significantly modified. The Coach does not over-react to single sessions — the programme is designed to deliver results over time and individual sessions should not trigger constant plan changes. Rebalancing happens when something meaningful changes (illness, injury, significant schedule disruption), not as a routine response to low RPE scores. If skipped due to fatigue or illness, the Coach absorbs the missed load and protects recovery. If skipped due to scheduling conflict, it may redistribute forward. Context is gathered at the next Weekly Session, not in the moment. In Coached Mode, rebalancing never silently alters Head-Coach-authored sessions — proposed changes to those are surfaced to the Head Coach as suggestions; only Coach-authored sessions remain freely rebalanceable.

**Weekly Session** — the primary structured ritual between Coach and athlete, occurring once per week. Three phases in sequence: (1) **Check-in** — the athlete reports bodily state for the week; (2) **Review** — the Coach and athlete evaluate the previous week's training using accumulated Session Feedback (Body Feedback, Mind Feedback, and any comments); (3) **Planning** — the Coach builds and presents the Week Plan for the coming week, then asks if it fits the athlete's life before adjusting. Planning order: Coach proposes first → asks "does this work for your week?" → adjusts if needed. The athlete does not declare constraints upfront before seeing the plan. Replaces the former daily Check-in and daily Session Negotiation as the primary interaction loop. The athlete can still trigger Session Negotiation outside this window to handle exceptions (illness, fatigue, schedule conflict). The Coach closes every Weekly Session with an open door and a motivational send-off.

**Expanded Week** — a week row of the Training Plan calendar toggled into its tall state: each day's dot becomes a Session Block laid out under its day column. Expansion is accordion-in-place — the month grid remains one surface, collapsed weeks stay as dot rows around it. Multiple weeks can be expanded simultaneously (e.g. the source and target weeks of an intended Cross-Week Move), and a header toggle expands or collapses all weeks at once. Tapping a week row toggles it.
_Avoid_: week view, week mode, zoomed week

**Session Move** — an athlete-initiated relocation of a session to a different day, performed by dragging the session block in the Training Plan calendar. A convenience action, not a coaching conversation — no Session Negotiation involved. Movable: planned, skipped, and unavailable sessions in the current and future weeks. Frozen: completed sessions, and every session in past weeks — the training record is immutable, and Week Rebalancing has already absorbed old missed load (making up last week's session is a Coach conversation, not a drag). Moving a skipped or unavailable session resets its status to planned — the athlete intends to do it on the new day. Drop targets: today and any future day of the current or future weeks. Past days are never drop targets — nothing is scheduled into the past. Fixed-constraint days are valid targets, silently: the constraint marker stays visible so the clash is self-evident, but no friction is added. Every Session Move is logged internally (session, from-day, to-day); within-week moves are silent and never raised by the Coach, but the log feeds Pattern Insight (e.g. an athlete who repeatedly drags Intensity to weekends is expressing a scheduling preference).
_Avoid_: reschedule, drag-and-drop edit, session swap

**Session Block** — the tappable, draggable chip representing one session inside an Expanded Week. Colour = Session Type, label = type and duration, style mirrors the dot status language (solid completed, outline planned, muted skipped/unavailable). Tapping a Session Block opens the Session Drawer; dragging it performs a Session Move. Rest renders as a muted block and is movable like any other, subject to Displacement.
_Avoid_: session chip, event card, calendar item

**Displacement** — the resolution rule when a Session Move conflicts with Rest. Rest is dominant: a Rest block is never displaced. A Rest block dropped onto a day with training flips that training to unavailable in place, badged, until the athlete re-places it — or the Coach resolves the dangling unavailable at the next Weekly Session. Training dropped onto a Rest day is parked: it lands as unavailable and returns to planned automatically if the Rest block is later moved away (or the athlete moves the training elsewhere first). Training onto training never conflicts — it forms a Double. Non-load Athlete Sessions (Mobility, Other toggled "not training") coexist with Rest — no displacement, no parking.
_Avoid_: conflict resolution, bump, overwrite, swap

**Double** — a day holding more than one active training session, created when the athlete drags a training block onto a day that already has training. Standard Ironman practice (morning swim, evening ride). Each session keeps its own identity, status, and Session Reflection — sessions are entities in their own right, not attributes of a day. Unlimited — the UI never caps how many sessions a day can hold: the Coach reads the final week shape and reacts at the Weekly Session if the stacking concerns it. Rest days cannot be part of a Double.
_Avoid_: two-a-day, stacked sessions, multi-session day

**Athlete Session** — a session authored by the athlete rather than the Coach, created via a "+" on a day inside an Expanded Week (opens the Session Drawer in create mode: type, optional duration, optional note). Types: Mobility, Strength, and Other — where Other carries a training / not-training toggle declaring whether it counts as load. Strength and Other-as-training follow all training rules (Doubles, Displacement parking, not offered on Rest days); Mobility and Other-as-not-training coexist with Rest. Otherwise a full session entity: movable, stackable, skippable, rateable via Session Reflection. Retro-logging: the "+" also works on past days of the current week — the session is created as already completed (done but forgotten), and the Session Reflection rating is offered immediately in the same create flow. Past weeks remain frozen; retro-logging does not reach them. Creation is silently logged, feeds Pattern Insight, and is visible to the Coach at the Weekly Session — never challenged in the moment. The Coach owns training load; Athlete Sessions are extras on top.
_Avoid_: custom workout, user session, manual entry

**Cross-Week Move** — a Session Move whose target day falls in a different week (Mon–Sun) than its source day. Changes weekly load distribution, so the Coach always reacts: every Cross-Week Move is logged (session, from-day, to-day, optional comment) and raised at the next Weekly Session — context is gathered there, not in the moment. The athlete's first two completed Cross-Week Moves trigger the Move Checkpoint; after that, moves happen without friction but are still logged.

**Move Checkpoint** — a popup intercepting an athlete's first two Cross-Week Moves. Speaks in the Coach's voice: explains the downside of shifting load across weeks, and offers an optional comment field (stored as Pushback Rationale) plus Move / Don't move buttons. Only completed moves count against the two-showing limit — cancelling doesn't. POC uses static copy; the product generates context-specific copy in the Coach's voice. Deliberate friction, informed by HI Pattern 3 (Frictional AI).
_Avoid_: warning modal, confirmation dialog, nag screen

**Session Type** — the category of a Planned Session, used for colour-coding in the Training Plan calendar. Types: Endurance (blue), Intensity (red), Tempo (amber), Recovery (green), Rest (grey). Dot style encodes status: solid = completed, outline = planned, muted = skipped.

## Training Structure

**Training Phase** — the current periodization stage, inferred by the Coach from race target date, elapsed training weeks, and athlete experience level. Surfaced to the athlete in plain language (e.g. "you're 10 weeks out, deep in your build"). The Coach owns phase planning — the athlete can understand and challenge it, but does not configure it manually. Phase definitions vary significantly by experience level: a first-timer's base phase differs substantially from a veteran's.

## Data Concepts

**Body Feedback** — the physical dimension of Session Reflection. Captures perceived effort, physical sensation, and fatigue after a session. Recorded using a RPE 1–10 scale with an illustrative visual element (emoji or colour). Body Feedback and Mind Feedback are separate inputs that combine to produce a coaching signal — physical fatigue calls for lower intensity or a discipline switch (e.g. swim instead of bike); mental fatigue calls for simpler, more manageable session formats.

**Mind Feedback** — the mental and emotional dimension of Session Reflection. Captures mood, motivation, and satisfaction after a session. Recorded using a RPE 1–10 scale alongside Body Feedback. Together, these are the app's primary differentiator — tracking subjective experience over time to detect patterns no objective metric can reveal.

**RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)** — the 1–10 scale used for Body Feedback and Mind Feedback. Industry standard in sports coaching for self-assessment of training sessions. 1 = minimal effort/impact, 10 = maximum. Both dimensions use this scale so ratings are consistent with the language athletes and coaches already use.

**Athlete Profile** — the accumulating model of the individual athlete. Seeded during the Onboarding Session and deepened continuously through Check-ins, Session Reflections, and Session Negotiation history.

## Language

**Athlete Language** — the language the athlete selects during the Onboarding Session. Applies to both the Coach's conversation and all UI labels, buttons, and navigation text. Set once during Onboarding and stored in the Athlete Profile. Changeable via Settings in V1+, not in MVP/POC. Default: English. Supported in MVP: English and Danish. Technical sports terminology (RPE, Zone 2, VO2max, FTP, etc.) is not translated — it remains in English regardless of language setting, as this is standard practice in Danish sports coaching. The Knowledge Oracle RAG handles the nuance of when English terms are used within Danish-language coaching.

## UI Navigation

**Navigation Drawer** — a toggleable sidebar that opens and overlaps ~20% of the main content area. Contains navigation buttons leading to the app's main views. Closing the drawer (or pressing a nav button) returns focus to the main content area. Analogous to Claude Code's Code / Cowork / Chat switcher.

**Session Drawer** — the single detail surface for sessions: a panel that slides in from the right, mirroring the Navigation Drawer's behavior (overlaps the content area, dismissed by tapping outside or closing). Opened by tapping a Session Block. Contents top-to-bottom: header (Session Type in its colour, status badge, full date), parameters (duration, zone), the Coach note (or the athlete's own note for an Athlete Session), Session Reflection display with Rate/Edit, and actions (skip/undo, unavailable/undo, Discuss with Coach). Also hosts the create form for new Athlete Sessions and the Weekly Session CTA when opened from the planning-day marker. Replaces the former inline calendar expansion — there is exactly one place session detail lives. Origin asymmetry: Athlete Sessions are editable and deletable here; Coach-planned and Head-Coach-prescribed session content is read-only and never deletable for the athlete — changing it is a coaching conversation, skipping it records reality. The athlete owns *placement* (drag), the Coach owns *content*.
_Avoid_: session popup, detail modal, side panel that switches based on the selected nav destination. Views include: Coach (the conversational interface), Training Plan, and others as they are added. Only one view is active at a time. The Coach view is the default.

**Coach View** — the primary view and the app's conversational interface. Contains the Weekly Session, any exception-triggered Session Negotiation, and Coach Chat. Reachable via the Coach button in the Navigation Drawer.

**Coach Chat** — an on-demand, open-ended conversation with the Coach, athlete-initiated at any time outside the Weekly Session. Scope is unrestricted: training questions, nutrition, equipment, race logistics, or anything else the athlete wants to discuss. The Coach still has full access to the Athlete Profile and session history in this mode. Not structured or Coach-led — the athlete sets the topic. Distinct from the Weekly Session (which is structured and Coach-led) and from Session Negotiation (which is exception-triggered and plan-focused). *Open question: GDPR/privacy implications of athlete free-text reaching a hosted LLM are unresolved — implementation deferred pending review.*

## Product Story

**Origin Story** — The athlete hears about the app from a training partner or friend in the triathlon community — a warm referral with a specific promise ("it's like having a real coach"). They arrive already half-sold. The first screen's job is not to convince but to live up to that promise. Primary drop-off risks: onboarding friction (too many fields before seeing value), anything that looks generic, and the privacy question ("what data are you collecting?"). The Climax of onboarding is the Coach's first message after the MCQ — immediately personal, immediately honest: *"Hello [Name], I'm your AI Coach. I look forward to helping you finish your goal and stand tall at [Race]."* This moment is where the athlete thinks "this is different" — not because it's magic, but because it knows their name and race from the first word, states its nature honestly, and frames the relationship around the athlete's end vision. The MCQ-to-conversation transition is acceptable — real coaches also need intake forms before the first session. The End: completing onboarding with a plan for the week and a felt sense of being heard. Acquisition channels in order: (1) athlete-to-athlete word of mouth in triathlon communities; (2) later, advertising and recommendations to aspiring athletes. Design implication: the referral loop is a product feature — athletes need an easy way to share. Onboarding tone: "let's get started", not "here's what this app does." The AI nature is stated upfront, not revealed through experience — owned confidently, not apologised for.
_Avoid_: growth hacking, viral loop, user acquisition funnel

**Concept Story** — For the serious amateur Ironman triathlete who can't afford a human coach but needs more than a training schedule, Biohacking Coach is an AI coaching app that learns you over time — your habits, your patterns, your life — and drives you to the start line in the best possible condition. Unlike Garmin or TrainingPeaks, it doesn't generate a plan and leave you alone. It coaches you. For the athlete who *does* have a human coach, Coached Mode makes that relationship stronger instead of competing with it — the Head Coach sets direction, the Coach holds the daily presence between human contact. A Head Coach is always a positive addition, never a requirement.

**Target Athlete** — Serious amateur Ironman triathlete. Training 8–15 hours/week alongside a full life (job, family, commitments). Has a fixed race date. Motivated by Achievement — improvement and performing at a high level. Has existing tools (Garmin, TrainingPeaks) but no personal coach. Primary resistance: *"This is just another training app"* and *"I don't want to upload my data to an AI."*

**End Vision** — Arriving at race day in the best possible condition, knowing a coach was driving them the whole season.

## Core Value Proposition

**Coaching Presence** — the felt quality that distinguishes this product from a training plan generator. The athlete experiences the Coach as continuously paying attention — remembering their habits, reading their patterns, adapting to their perspective — even between sessions. The BOOM moment is not a single feature: it is the accumulation of micro-moments across weeks where the athlete realises the Coach has been holding their context the whole time. The ideal end-state: the Coach acts on what the athlete needs before the athlete has to articulate it. This is the product's primary differentiator against generic training apps (which generate plans but do not remember) and human coaches (who remember but are inaccessible). Closely expressed through Pattern Insight, Reliance Calibration, Tone Adaptation, and Peer Authority.
_Avoid_: personalisation, AI memory, smart coaching

**Presence Arc** — the progression from honest cold start to full Coaching Presence over time. The arc runs across four stages, each with a distinct Weekly Session posture: (1) **Week 1** — the Coach welcomes the athlete into the programme, is honest it knows them only from onboarding, and asks one grounding question about where they are physically right now. No review phase — there is nothing to review yet. (2) **Week 2** — "how did the first week go?" — a concrete debrief of the first week, not a generic check-in. The Coach shows it remembers what was said in session one. (3) **Week 3** — still early, but the Coach begins referencing specific things from the past two weeks. Declares uncertainty where pattern evidence is thin. (4) **Week 4+** — standard Weekly Session arc: Reflective Prompt, Review, Planning. Pattern Insights can now be surfaced aloud if consistent across at least two weeks. The arc is earned, not simulated. Design implication: the guided tour and onboarding should set this expectation explicitly — "the more you engage, the better the Coach knows you" — rather than overpromising on day one.
_Avoid_: cold start problem, ramp-up period, personalisation over time

## Interaction Patterns

**Contextual Signals** — objective data points the Coach reads alongside Session Feedback and Pushback Rationale. In MVP: self-reported weekly during Check-in (sleep quality, perceived energy, physical condition, any notable health signals). In V2: wearable-sourced data aggregated automatically across the week. Cross-referenced against athlete self-assessments to detect confirmed signals (self-report matches data) and divergences (self-report conflicts with data — often the more valuable coaching finding).

**Pattern Insight** — a Coach-detected cross-variable pattern that shapes the Coach's weekly planning over time. Emerges from correlating Body Feedback, Mind Feedback, session comments, and weekly Check-in signals across multiple weeks. Patterns operate at a weekly granularity rather than per-session — e.g. "Body Feedback consistently low during peak load weeks", "Mind Feedback drops when Check-in sleep quality is poor", "athlete's self-assessed energy consistently underestimates actual session performance". Two modes: (1) **Silent use** — early or weak patterns inform the Week Plan invisibly without mention; (2) **Named observation** — when a pattern is consistent across multiple weeks, the Coach surfaces it during the Weekly Session as an observation and normalisation, never as criticism or data reporting. Example framing: "I've noticed intensity sessions tend to score lower on your mind rating — that's common in this phase. Worth talking about?" The athlete may also ask "why did you plan it this way?" at any point, prompting a natural-language explanation framed as coaching intuition. Informed by HI Pattern 5 (Metacognitive Reflection).

**Pushback Rationale** — the athlete's stated reason for deviating from the Week Plan during an exception-triggered Session Negotiation. Stored as part of the Athlete Profile. Captured via a single Coach follow-up ("What's making you want to dial it back — body or head?"), or via the Move Checkpoint's optional comment field during a Cross-Week Move. Now a rarer signal than in the daily model — meaningful precisely because it represents a deliberate deviation, not routine negotiation. Cross-referenced against Session Feedback and Check-in signals to identify confirmed signals and divergences. Informed by HI Pattern 7 (Judgment & Preference Explicitation).

**Reflective Prompt** — a set of Coach-initiated questions at the start of the Weekly Session that asks the athlete to self-assess before the Coach shares its read of the week. Used to build body awareness, surface the athlete's own perception of their needs and progress, and prevent Coach dependency. The Coach then compares the athlete's self-assessment against the Session Feedback data — the tension between the two is a key coaching signal. Topics may span multiple dimensions: physical state, energy and recovery, motivation and mental load, perceived progress, and any health signals from the week. Exact topic set to be refined with domain expert input. Informed by HI Pattern 3 (Frictional AI).

**Session Negotiation** — an exception-triggered Coach dialogue that occurs when the athlete actively wants to change a Planned Session (illness, fatigue, wanting a different session type). Distinct from a skip: if the athlete simply cannot do a session, they mark it as not done without any required explanation — context comes in the next Weekly Session. Session Negotiation is for when the athlete wants to negotiate an alternative on that day. The Coach reads the reason, adjusts the session, and notes it for Week Rebalancing assessment. Not a daily default — Session Negotiation is the escape hatch, not the primary interaction loop.

**Coach Awareness** — the athlete-visible acknowledgement of their emotional state by the Coach. The surface expression of Tone Adaptation. Example: "I can see you're low energy today — I'll keep this brief." Builds trust by showing the Coach is listening.

**Progressive Disclosure** — the Coach-initiated unlocking of deeper features (trend analysis, biometric overlays) as the athlete builds history and engagement. Surfaced conversationally by the Coach, not via a settings toggle.

**Monthly Review Session** — a deeper, less frequent conversation between Coach and athlete focused on the big picture: Training Phase progress, trajectory, and longer-term adjustments. Complements the Weekly Session. Occurs approximately once per month. Not structured around a single week's data — instead zooms out to assess the training block as a whole. V1 feature; not in MVP scope.

**Coaching Closing Move** — the standard way a Coach ends a Weekly Session: (1) open door ("write if you have any questions during the week, I'm here"), (2) motivational send-off ("give it your all"). This formula is used consistently at the end of every Weekly Session. It signals availability without demanding check-ins.

**Guided Tour** — the mechanism by which new athletes learn how to use the product. Delivered entirely through the Coach's voice at contextually appropriate moments — never as UI overlays, tooltips, or arrow-based walkthroughs. The Coach teaches the athlete how the product works as a natural extension of the coaching relationship. Three principles: (1) the tour is woven into real coaching moments, not added on top of them; (2) the Coach introduces a feature only at the moment the athlete needs it; (3) no feature is introduced twice — if the athlete saw it, they saw it. The guided tour is complete by the end of week 2, delivered across two natural moments: the First Weekly Session Closing and the Second Weekly Session opening.
_Avoid_: product tour, onboarding overlay, feature walkthrough, tooltip tour

**Week 1 BOOM** — the moment in the first week that makes the Coach the product's selling point: the first time the athlete uses Coach Chat mid-week. They have a question — nutrition, a session they're unsure about, anything — and the Coach answers with full context from onboarding and the Week Plan from the first word. No training app does this. Session Reflection is table stakes (Garmin and TrainingPeaks both have it) — not a differentiator. The First Weekly Session Closing must actively seed the Coach Chat habit with a concrete example of what to ask, not just name the feature: "If a question comes up this week — nutrition before the long run, a session you're unsure about, how you're feeling — come find me in Coach Chat." This directly drives Coach Engagement Rate, the product's primary health metric.
_Avoid_: mid-week check-in, chatbot, in-app messaging

**First Weekly Session Closing** — the specific extended version of the Coaching Closing Move used at the end of the athlete's very first Weekly Session. In addition to the standard open door and send-off, the Coach introduces three features in plain language: (1) Training Plan — "Your plan is in the Training Plan. After each session, tap it in the calendar and give me your body and mind rating — ten seconds, and that's how I start learning what works for you." (2) Equipment — "If you add your gear in the Equipment tab, I can give you more specific advice — bike, shoes, watch, whatever you're training with." (3) Glossary — "If you come across terms you don't recognise, the Glossary tab has them all." Then the escape hatch: "If something comes up mid-week, you can find me in Coach Chat." The athlete leaves the first session knowing what to do next, where their plan lives, and what to do if something goes wrong. Tone: matter-of-fact, not a product tour — the Coach is orienting the athlete, not selling features.
_Avoid_: feature list, app tutorial, onboarding checklist

**Equipment Nudge** — a Coach-initiated mention of the Equipment tab if the athlete has not added any gear by the second or third Weekly Session. Triggered by the absence of equipment data in the athlete's profile. Framed as a coaching need, not a product prompt: "I still don't know what you're training on — if you add your bike and shoes in the Equipment tab, I can be more specific with my advice." Delivered once, naturally within the Weekly Session review or planning phase. Not repeated after that — if the athlete hasn't added equipment after two nudges, the Coach adapts without it.
_Avoid_: re-engagement prompt, feature reminder, push notification

**Trajectory Projection** — a Knowledge Oracle-backed projection of the athlete's development path, surfaced by the Coach as a motivational signal. Shape varies by experience level: for beginners, aspirational ("what consistent Ironman training can unlock"); for veterans, personal ("what you have built and where you are headed"). Used to reinforce belief and consistency, not as a performance dashboard.
